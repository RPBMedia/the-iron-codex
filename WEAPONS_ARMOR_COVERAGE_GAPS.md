# Weapons & Armor — coverage gap analysis (M8)

**Status: proposal. Nothing here is built. M9 is an approval gate — see the end.**

The archive holds 42 Weapons & Armor articles, all now at the documented standard.
This analysis asks a different question: *what is missing?*

The method is deliberately not "what feels absent". Every candidate below was
found by scanning what the archive's own prose already references, across all
collections, and checking whether an article exists for it. A gap that the
archive keeps pointing at is a gap the archive has already admitted to.

## Tier 1 — the archive currently contradicts itself

These are not "nice to have". In each case the archive discusses the thing
repeatedly, in some cases as the decisive factor in a battle it covers in full,
and has no article for it.

### 1. Gunpowder artillery (bombard / siege cannon)

**Referenced in 16 articles**, including both battles that close the Codex era.
The Battle of Castillon in 1453 was decided by French guns in a fortified camp, and
the Fall of Constantinople in the same year by Ottoman bombards against the
Theodosian Walls. The archive narrates both as artillery victories and then offers
the reader nowhere to go.

This is the single worst gap in the collection.

### 2. Siege engines (trebuchet, mangonel)

**Zero coverage anywhere in the archive** — one passing mention of a mangonel at
the Siege of Lisbon, and the word "trebuchet" does not appear once. The archive
contains a substantial number of siege articles, including the Mongol sieges of
Ryazan, Vladimir and Kyiv, and the machines that decided them are invisible.

### 3. Pike

**17 mentions in Weapons & Armor prose alone.** The spear article names the pike as
its own descendant; the halberd article explains that halberdiers supported pike
blocks; the lance article explains that pikes ended the couched charge. It is the
weapon that took the battlefield back from cavalry, and it is the hole all three
of those articles are built around.

### 4. Hand cannon (handgonne)

The crossbow and longbow articles both end by naming the handgun as the thing that
replaced them, and both make the same argument — that a weapon usable after a
fortnight's training beats one requiring a decade. That argument currently
terminates in a weapon the archive does not describe.

## Tier 4 — components, deliberately NOT proposed as articles

These are referenced, sometimes often, but are parts of a system already covered by
an existing article. Splitting them out would fragment the collection rather than
deepen it. Recorded here so the decision is visible rather than accidental:

**aventail** (16 mentions — covered in bascinet), **breastplate** (14 — covered in
plate-armor), **bevor** (8 — covered in sallet), **cervelliere**, **jack of plate**,
**bollock dagger**, **voulge / bardiche / glaive**, **francisca**, **caltrop**,
**sling**.

If any of these is wanted as a standalone, say so — the argument for **breastplate**
in particular is not weak.

## Named-artifact candidates

The M5 rewrite cites these real objects by name and could carry any of them as a
full named-artifact article, in the style of the Sutton Hoo helmet:

- **Gjermundbu helmet** — the only substantially complete Viking-age helmet known
- **Coppergate helmet** — the York Anglo-Saxon helmet
- **Mammen axe** — silver-inlaid, National Museum of Denmark
- **Seax of Beagnoth** — British Museum; the only known object carrying the complete futhorc
- **Conyers Falchion** — Durham; still a land-tenure token rather than a museum piece
- **Coventry Sallet** — Herbert Art Gallery and Museum; now the sallet article's secondary image

## Recommendation

Build **Tier 1 (4 articles)**. The archive actively contradicts itself without
them, and two of the four are needed by battles it already narrates as decided by
that technology.

**Tier 2 (5 articles)** is a strong second phase and would leave the collection
genuinely complete for the period.

**Tier 3** and the named artifacts are discretionary and can wait behind Track A
and Track C — with one exception: **lamellar and scale armour is a dependency of
Track A**, so if the Byzantine expansion is next, that article should move up.

Each article costs roughly what an M5 rewrite did: 8 sections, 24 paragraphs, a
sourced principal image meeting the full-object and condition standards, 5+ related
entries, 3+ sources.

**The image requirement is the real cost, not the prose.** Artillery and siege
engines are the hardest subjects in the collection to illustrate honestly — surviving
medieval bombards exist (Mons Meg, Faule Grete, the Dardanelles Gun), but trebuchets
survive only as modern reconstructions, which the standard permits provided the
caption leads with it.
