# IronCodex Content Guidelines

IronCodex articles should read like serious historical archive entries, not reusable medieval-themed templates. Every paragraph should teach the reader something specific about the subject.

## Core Rules

- Articles must be historically specific and informative.
- Do not write vague template prose that could apply to many medieval subjects.
- Every section must contain concrete facts specific to the article: named people, places, dates, dynasties, conflicts, institutions, causes, and consequences.
- Do not invent certainty. Use careful language for disputed or poorly attested material.
- Use phrases such as "probably", "possibly", "traditionally", "according to later tradition", "medieval sources claim", "modern historians treat this cautiously", or "the chronology is uncertain" when the evidence requires it.
- No placeholder images. Every article image must be real, relevant, and attributed.
- Image captions must explain whether an image is contemporary, later, symbolic, reconstructed, romanticized, or a modern photograph.
- Sources and further reading should be concrete and linkable wherever possible.
- Person timelines must contain at least five events.
- Key achievements must include descriptions, not only titles.
- Internal links should connect major people, events, polities, locations, artifacts, documents, and institutions.
- New linked entities should become real articles with useful content, not placeholder pages.

## Avoid Vague Filler

Do not use broad phrases unless they are immediately followed by concrete subject-specific detail. Suspicious phrases include:

- "developed in the context of regional medieval politics"
- "was shaped by lordship, military pressure, dynastic claims"
- "played an important role"
- "was connected to warfare and diplomacy"
- "became significant over time"
- "had an impact on later history"
- "control of land and routes mattered"
- "the balance between central power and regional autonomy changed over time"
- "its legacy continued in later centuries"
- "this figure was influential"
- "this artifact was meaningful"

## Better Pattern

Bad:

> Kingdom of Wessex developed in the context of regional medieval politics.

Good:

> Wessex rose from an Anglo-Saxon kingdom in southern Britain into the leading power against Viking armies in the ninth century. Under Alfred the Great and his successors, its military reforms, fortified burhs, and royal administration became the foundation for the later Kingdom of England.

Bad:

> The battle was important because it affected medieval politics.

Good:

> The Battle of Legnano weakened Frederick Barbarossa's attempt to impose imperial authority on the Lombard communes. The defeat helped lead to the Peace of Constance in 1183, which recognized important communal liberties in northern Italy while preserving imperial prestige in form.

Bad:

> The ruler was influential and shaped his realm.

Good:

> Harald Bluetooth strengthened Danish kingship through the Jelling dynasty, promoted Christianity as royal ideology, and left the Jelling stones as a public statement of family power, conversion, and rule over Denmark and Norway.

## Quality Checklist

Before adding or editing an article, confirm:

- The overview explains who or what the subject was, why it matters, and what changed because of it.
- Each section has at least one concrete date, person, place, institution, conflict, or consequence when evidence allows.
- Uncertain evidence is marked honestly.
- Images are relevant and attributed.
- Source entries use specific URLs, not generic homepages.
- Related entries and internal links point to real IronCodex pages.
- The article would still make sense if removed from a template and read on its own.
